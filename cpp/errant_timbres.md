Errant timbres (2025) is a series of long-duration, fixed-media drone compositions employing intervened soundscapes and modular synthesis techniques for timbral alteration  that test the boundaries between field recording, soundscape, drone, and noise.

The work is based around a series of recordings I have made in past years in liminal territories  in Colombia (the northern Amazon, the Magdalena river, the Caribbean Coast, and high-mountain páramo ecosystems) and Canada (Signal Hill in St. John’s Newfoundland, BIC and Mont Tremblant National Parks in Quebec), where the boundaries between human and natural ecosystems blur to the point of non-existence. Using a series of bespoke analog and digital tools, the project explores techniques for timbral transfer using filter-banks and FFT to analyze the timbre of one sound and imprint it onto another sound, producing soundscapes that make audible the tragic entwinement of the natural and the human in these ecosystems.

By exploring the technique of timbral transfer, I address lasting issues in soundscape recording practices, where a concern with documenting and preserving the natural environment is in tension with the role of recording technology in distancing us from these very environments. As Murray Schafer’s notion of schizophonia suggests, recording always removes a sound from its source, making it an errant timbre. What happens to natural sounds as they are captured and displaced by recording machines? How can this tension be reconciled with our daily experience of a changing soundscape? How can field recordings help us listen otherwise to a world in crisis?
